# Introduction

The Vantage sensor node is the field unit of a flood-monitoring system. It is built from two boards connected by
one cable: the sensor board (see VNT-001) and the power board described here.

The power board keeps the node running from a single 18650 lithium-ion cell charged by a small solar panel. It
provides the 3.3 V supply of the ESP32-S3, a 5 V supply for the sensors that the ESP32-S3 switches on only while
it measures, and two current monitors that report the solar and battery currents over I2C. The board is built
mainly from ready-made modules, so that a faulty function can be repaired by replacing one module.

# 1 Scope

This document describes the circuit of the Vantage power board, Rev A, its interfaces and configuration, and
specifies the tests that a power board shall pass before it is connected to a sensor board.

It does not cover the sensor board (see VNT-001), the firmware, the solar panel mounting or the enclosure.

# 3 Terms and definitions

For the purposes of this document, the terms and definitions given in VNT-001 and the following apply.

### 3.1

**VBAT**

internal supply rail of the power board, at battery voltage, from which the 3.3 V and 5 V supplies are made

### 3.2

**maximum power point tracking**

charger function that keeps the solar panel at the voltage at which it delivers the most power

### 3.3

**battery management system**

protection circuit that disconnects the cell on over-charge, over-discharge, over-current and short circuit

### 3.4

**ideal diode**

circuit that passes current in one direction with a very small voltage drop and blocks reverse current

### 3.5

**sensor 5 V**

switched 5 V supply for the sensors, delivered to J1 pin 4

# 5 Description

## 5.1 General

The power distribution is shown in Figure 1. The solar input and the battery both feed the VBAT rail. The 3.3 V
supply and the sensor 5 V are made from VBAT.

![Power distribution](figures/power_blocks.svg){: .wide}

Figure 1 — Power distribution of the power board

## 5.2 Solar input

The solar panel connects to J2. D1 (SMAJ9.0CA) clamps voltage surges on the panel cable. D2 (SS34) blocks
reverse current and protects against a reversed panel. U1, an INA226 module at I2C address 0x40, measures the
panel voltage after D2 and the panel current. M1, a CN3791 MPPT charger module for a 6 V panel, charges the cell
through the VBAT rail.

## 5.3 Battery

The cell BT1 plugs into a 2-way JST XH header. M2, a 1S protection module, disconnects the negative side of the
cell on a fault; its B+ and P+ terminals are joined. F1 (PTC, 1.5 A) limits the battery current. JP1 (BAT_ISO)
opens the battery path for storage, transport and current measurement. U2, an INA226 module at I2C address 0x44,
measures the battery voltage and the battery current in both directions: positive while the cell discharges into
VBAT, negative while M1 charges it.

C1 (220 µF) and C2 (100 nF) decouple VBAT. TP1 gives access to VBAT.

## 5.4 3.3 V supply

M3, a TPS63020 buck-boost module with 3.3 V output, keeps the 3.3 V supply steady over the whole cell voltage
range. U3 (LM66100) is an ideal diode between M3 and the output; it stops the 3.3 V of the dev board, when the
dev board is on USB, from flowing back into M3. Its enable input is tied to its output, which gives reverse
blocking. C3 (220 µF) and C4 (100 nF) decouple the output. TP2 gives access to the 3.3 V supply, which goes to
J1 pin 6, to the INA226 modules, to the I2C pull-ups and to J3.

## 5.5 Sensor 5 V

U4 (TPS2051B) is a current-limited switch between VBAT and M4, a 5 V boost module. Its enable input is
SENS_EN from J1 pin 3, with a 10 kΩ pull-down (R1), so the sensor 5 V is off unless the ESP32-S3 drives SENS_EN
high. C5 (100 nF), C6 (10 µF), C7 (220 µF) and C8 (100 nF) decouple the switch and the boost module. TP3 gives
access to the sensor 5 V, which goes to J1 pin 4.

## 5.6 I2C bus

The I2C bus runs from the sensor board through J1 pins 1 and 2 to U1 and U2. R2 and R3 (4.7 kΩ) are the only
pull-up resistors on the bus. J3 brings the bus out for an extra I2C device or a test adapter.

# 6 Interfaces and configuration

## 6.1 Connectors and test points

Table 1 — Connectors and test points

| Ref. | Function | Pins |
|---|---|---|
| J1 | Sensor board connection, JST XH 6-way, vertical | 1 I2C_SDA, 2 I2C_SCL, 3 SENS_EN, 4 sensor 5 V, 5 GND, 6 +3V3 |
| J2 | Solar panel, 2-way screw terminal 5.08 mm | 1 panel +, 2 panel − |
| BT1 | 18650 cell lead, JST XH 2-way | 1 cell +, 2 cell − |
| J3 | I2C expansion, 4-way male header 2.54 mm | 1 GND, 2 +3V3, 3 I2C_SDA, 4 I2C_SCL |
| TP1 | VBAT | — |
| TP2 | +3V3 | — |
| TP3 | Sensor 5 V | — |
| TP4 | GND | — |

The pin assignment of J1 shall match J1 of the sensor board (VNT-001, Annex C). The cable shall be wired pin 1
to pin 1 (VNT-001, 10.3).

## 6.2 Jumper JP1 (BAT_ISO)

JP1 shall be fitted for operation. JP1 may be opened to isolate the cell for storage or transport, or replaced by
an ammeter to measure the battery current.

The board should not be run from the solar panel with JP1 open, because M1 would then work without a cell.

## 6.3 Module settings

The modules shall be set as follows before they are fitted:

1. U2 shall be set to I2C address 0x44 with the address pads of the module (A1 to VS, A0 to GND). U1 stays at the
   default address 0x40 (A1 and A0 to GND).
2. M1 shall be the version for a 6 V panel, or its maximum power point shall be set for the panel in use as given
   in the module documentation.
3. The charge current of M1 shall not exceed the rating of the cell. It should not exceed 0.8 A, the measuring
   range of an INA226 module with a 0.1 Ω shunt (see Annex B).
4. M3 shall be a fixed 3.3 V output version. M4 shall have a 5 V output and accept an input from 3.0 V to 4.2 V.

# 7 Safety

WARNING — A lithium-ion cell can deliver very high current. Do not short its terminals or its lead. Connect the
cell last and disconnect it first. Do not use or charge a damaged, swollen or deeply discharged cell.

WARNING — Check the polarity of the cell lead and of the panel cable before connecting them.

# 8 Prototype construction

Until the printed circuit board is released, a prototype may be built on stripboard or perforated board following
the schematic. In that case:

- the battery and VBAT paths (BT1, M2, F1, JP1, U2, M1 output, M3 and U4 inputs) shall be wired with copper of at
  least 0.5 mm² cross-section;
- the ground connections of all modules shall return to one common ground point near C1;
- U3 and U4 are small surface-mount parts (SC-70 and SOT-23) and should be mounted on adapter boards;
- J1 shall be wired exactly as in Table 1.

# 9 Tests

## 9.1 General

The tests shall be made in the order of this clause, before the power board is connected to a sensor board.
Equipment: a bench supply with an adjustable current limit, a digital multimeter, and an I2C controller (for
example the sensor board connected through a test cable, or a USB I2C adapter on J3). The results should be
recorded on the form in Annex C.

## 9.2 Visual and resistance checks

With no cell and no panel connected, check that all modules are fitted in the right orientation, and that the
resistance from each of TP1, TP2 and TP3 to TP4 is above 1 kΩ once the capacitors have charged.

## 9.3 Supply tests without the cell

Open JP1. Set the bench supply to 3.7 V with a current limit of 0.3 A and connect it between TP1 (+) and TP4 (−).
The results shall be as given in Table 2.

Table 2 — Supply tests

| No. | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| S-1 | Supply current, SENS_EN open | Below 50 mA |
| S-2 | Voltage TP2 to TP4 | 3.20 V to 3.40 V |
| S-3 | Voltage TP3 to TP4, SENS_EN (J1-3) open | Below 0.3 V |
| S-4 | Voltage TP3 to TP4, SENS_EN (J1-3) connected to +3V3 (J3-2) | 4.75 V to 5.25 V |
| S-5 | Voltage TP2 to TP4 with the bench supply set to 3.0 V, then 4.2 V | 3.20 V to 3.40 V at both settings |

## 9.4 I2C tests

With the bench supply still connected, connect the I2C controller and scan the bus. The results shall be as given
in Table 3.

Table 3 — I2C tests

| No. | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| I-1 | Bus scan | Devices found at 0x40 and 0x44, and no others |
| I-2 | U1 bus voltage and current, no panel | Close to 0 V and 0 mA |

## 9.5 Tests with the cell and the panel

Disconnect the bench supply. Check that the cell voltage is between 3.0 V and 4.2 V and that the polarity of its
lead is correct, plug it into BT1 and fit JP1. Then connect the panel in daylight, or a bench supply set to 6.0 V
with a current limit of 0.5 A, to J2. The results shall be as given in Table 4.

Table 4 — Tests with the cell and the panel

| No. | Test | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| B-1 | U2 bus voltage compared with the voltage TP1 to TP4 | Within ± 0.05 V |
| B-2 | U2 current, no panel, SENS_EN open | Positive (discharge), below 50 mA |
| B-3 | U1 bus voltage with the panel connected | Panel voltage less 0.3 V to 0.5 V (drop of D2) |
| B-4 | U2 current with the panel connected and M1 charging | Negative (charging) |

## 9.6 Test with the sensor board

Connect the sensor board as specified in VNT-001, 10.3 and carry out the field-mode tests of VNT-001, 11.6.

# Annex B (informative) Current monitors

Common INA226 modules carry a 0.1 Ω shunt resistor, marked R100. The INA226 measures up to 81.92 mV across the
shunt, so the measuring range is about ± 0.8 A. Table B.1 gives the settings that suit this shunt.

Table B.1 — INA226 settings for a 0.1 Ω shunt

| Item | U1 (solar) | U2 (battery) |
|---|---|---|
| I2C address | 0x40 | 0x44 |
| Shunt resistance | 0.1 Ω | 0.1 Ω |
| Current resolution (current LSB) | 0.1 mA | 0.1 mA |
| Calibration register (0.00512 / (current LSB × shunt)) | 512 | 512 |
| Bus voltage measured at | Panel side, after D2 | Battery side, after JP1 |
| Sign of the current | Positive from the panel | Positive on discharge, negative on charge |

NOTE If a module carries another shunt value, recalculate the calibration register with the formula in the
table and check the measuring range against the charge current of M1.
